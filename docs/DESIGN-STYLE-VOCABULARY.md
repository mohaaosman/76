# Design Style Vocabulary — Project Memory

**Purpose:** Shared visual vocabulary for this project ("Max - The Architect"). Use these named styles to anchor design proposals, AI prompts, and moodboards with precision instead of vague adjectives. Derived from Himanshu Bhardwaj's "50 Design Styles Every Designer Should Know for Better Prompting" (UX Planet, Aug 2025), restructured and extended for our anti-slop, industry-first workflow.

**How to use in this project:**

1. During Step 2 (Design Proposal), name 1–2 styles per option — never "modern and clean," always e.g. "Japandi base with Bauhaus grid discipline."
2. Hybridize deliberately: pick ONE dominant style for structure + ONE accent style for personality. More than two reads as noise.
3. Cross-check against the Industry Fit Map below before proposing. A style that fights the domain's mental model is auto-rejected (Anti-Slop Law #3).
4. In prompts, lead with the style name, then constrain: "Neo-Brutalist dashboard UI, restrained palette, dense data tables, no decorative gradients."

## FAMILY 1 — Classical & Ornamental

*Heritage, authority, luxury. Use sparingly in UI — best as brand/marketing surfaces, not product screens.*

**1. Neoclassical** — Greco-Roman order and symmetry. Serifs, columns, laurels, marble, muted golds. → Museums, heritage brands, academia, luxury. Feels formal and timeless.

**2. Baroque** — Theatrical excess: flourishes, deep shadow, gold leaf, rich contrast. → Premium packaging, event invitations, lavish editorial. Too heavy for interfaces.

**3. Filigree** — Fine metallic linework, lace-like ornament, monogram lettering. → Heritage branding, luxury packaging, certificates.

**4. Acanthus** — Classical foliage ornament (Corinthian leaf motifs), symmetrical vines, stone + green + gold. → Weddings, art institutions, premium product.

**5. Victorian** — 19th-century ornate maximalism: damask, gilded frames, deep reds/greens, heavy florals. → Book covers, tea/vintage brands, historical storytelling.

**6. Luxury Typography** — Type IS the identity: refined serifs, wide tracking, monochrome + gold foil, bespoke ligatures. → High fashion, beauty, premium editorial. The most UI-transferable of this family.

## FAMILY 2 — Soft & Atmospheric

*Calm, wonder, transcendence. Natural fit for wellness, mindfulness, spiritual — matches our Health & Wellness directive (calm color theory, non-punitive tone).*

**7. Aurora** — Northern-lights energy: iridescent flowing gradients, soft glows, translucent waves. → Wellness apps, ambient tech launches, cosmic branding. ⚠ Border-of-slop: only with intent, never as default gradient wash.

**8. Ethereal** — Weightless and misty: pale pastels, gauzy overlays, low contrast, feathered shadow, delicate serifs. → Spiritual brands, high fashion, mindfulness. ⚠ Watch contrast accessibility.

**9. Japandi** — Japanese minimalism × Scandinavian function: light wood, beige/gray, soft curves, decluttered. → Lifestyle, home, meditation, calm productivity tools. Excellent UI style.

**10. Wabi Sabi** — Beauty of imperfection: earthy tones, rough organic textures, asymmetry, quiet restraint. → Wellness, tea/craft brands, mindful interfaces.

**11. Light Academia** — Sunlit classicism: cream neutrals, serifs, linen, books-and-libraries romance. → Journals, study/reading apps, refined lifestyle.

## FAMILY 3 — Modernist & Structural

*Function-first backbone styles. Primary sources for ERP/CRM, dashboards, tooling — matches our Enterprise directive (density, hierarchy, zero clutter).*

**12. Bauhaus** — Form follows function: strict grids, geometric primitives, red/blue/yellow, sans-serif economy. → Brand systems, product design, architecture. Great structural DNA for any dense UI.

**13. Utilitarian** — Military-industrial clarity: grids, monospace/industrial type, muted tones, zero decoration. → Manuals, signage, technical/ops interfaces. Ideal ERP undertone.

**14. Brutalism** — Raw and norm-breaking: monospace, grayscale, harsh edges, default-looking controls. → Artist portfolios, counterculture, experimental sites. Not for enterprise workflows.

**15. Neo-Brutalism** — Brutalism refined for usability: bold flat color, oversized type, stark blocks, purposeful asymmetry, real UI hygiene. → Portfolios, agencies, digital magazines, bold consumer tools. Trending and usable.

**16. Bento Box** — Content in modular rounded compartments: labeled tiles, subtle shadow, micro-animation, neutral palette. → Dashboards, portfolios, productivity. Trending; good for feature overviews and KPI grids.

**17. Modular Typography** — Letterforms built on grids/blocks, systematic and rhythmic. → Poster systems, experimental branding, identity systems.

**18. Mid-Century** — 1940s–60s optimism: clean lines, organic-geometric shapes (boomerangs), warm retro palette. → Furniture, lifestyle, editorial with warmth.

**19. Glassmorphism** — Frosted-glass layers: blur, semi-transparency, soft depth, subtle tint. → OS-style UI, product landing pages, fintech cards. ⚠ Slop-adjacent when stacked on purple mesh gradients — use over disciplined layouts only.

## FAMILY 4 — Retro-Tech & Internet-Native

*Nostalgic digital culture. Strong for gaming, music, youth, tech-revival branding.*

**20. Pixel Art** — 8/16-bit grid graphics, limited palettes. → Indie games, retro events, playful dev branding.

**21. Y2K** — Late-90s/2000s cyber gloss: chrome, iridescence, pixel fonts, matrix grids. → Fashion, tech-throwback, edgy campaigns.

**22. Neo Frutiger Aero** — 2000s "aero" revival: aqua blues, glossy bubbles, translucency, friendly rounded UI. → Playful tech, consumer fintech, Web3-adjacent brands.

**23. Synthwave** — 80s neon retro-future: grid horizons, VHS grain, chrome 3D type, purple/cyan glow. → Music, arcade, nostalgia products. ⚠ Neon-glow slop risk — deliberate use only.

**24. Vaporwave** — Ironic surreal nostalgia: pastel pink/purple, glitch, Greek busts, Japanese text, lo-fi UI relics. → Indie music, zines, satire.

**25. Cybercore** — Hacker/sci-fi density: neon on black/green, glitch, code motifs. → Gaming, security/tech events, dystopian themes. ⚠ Never the default for real security products — clarity beats theater.

## FAMILY 5 — Bold Graphic & Pop

*Loud, energetic, attention-first. Marketing and campaign surfaces.*

**26. Memphis** — 1980s anti-rules play: squiggles, clashing primaries, abstract geometry. → Creative brands, event posters, youth products.

**27. Pop Art** — Comic/advertising language: Ben-Day dots, bold outlines, primaries, speech bubbles. → Pop campaigns, retail, fashion.

**28. Graffiti** — Street energy: spray textures, drips, concrete, freestyle letters. → Streetwear, festivals, urban youth campaigns.

**29. Kitsch** — Ironic "bad taste": clashing prints, dated fonts, plastic shine, sentimental excess. → Parody, Gen Z fashion, nostalgic remix.

## FAMILY 6 — Craft, Nostalgic & Lifestyle

*Handmade warmth and memory. Lifestyle, food, home, personal products.*

**30. Bohemian** — Eclectic global-artisan layering: jewel tones, mandalas, textiles, script type. → Handmade, travel, artisanal markets.

**31. Shabby Chic** — Distressed vintage femininity: whitewash, faded florals, lace, cursive, soft pink/blue. → Weddings, vintage boutiques, decor.

**32. Farmhouse / Cottagecore** — Romanticized rural slow living: gingham, florals, wood, antique finishes, hand-drawn type. → Recipe/lifestyle content, rustic home brands.

**33. Scrapbook** — Collaged memory-keeping: paper texture, washi tape, Polaroid frames, stickers, handwriting. → Journaling apps, memory products, education.

**34. Coquette** — Flirty vintage-feminine: baby pink, ribbons, pearls, hearts, dainty serifs. → Beauty, fashion social content, romantic lifestyle.

**35. Kawaii** — Japanese cuteness: pastels, round characters, blush cheeks, handwritten type. → Kids' products, stationery, whimsical brands.

**36. Anthropomorphic** — Human traits on objects/animals: expressive faces, limbs on things, friendly rounded characters. → Mascots, children's UX, gamified onboarding, approachable empty-states.

## FAMILY 7 — Dark & Dramatic

*Mystery, weight, intensity. Entertainment, fantasy, editorial drama.*

**37. Gothic** — Medieval dark romanticism: blackletter, stained glass, stone, deep purple/black. → Fantasy covers, music, alt fashion.

**38. Tenebrism** — Extreme chiaroscuro: spotlight subjects on near-black, oil-painting depth. → Cinematic posters, gothic art, dramatic storytelling.

**39. Dark Magic Academia** — Scholarly occult: candles, spellbooks, etchings, black/gold, calligraphic serifs. → Fantasy games, book communities, "witchy" brands.

**40. Steampunk** — Victorian × industrial sci-fi: brass, cogs, leather, steam motifs. → Games, books, themed experiences.

**41. Mystical Western** — Cowboy × occult: tarot and sun/moon symbols, desert palette, leather, western serifs. → Crystal/tarot apps, moody music, indie brands.

## FAMILY 8 — Thematic & Regional

*Place-rooted identity systems.*

**42. South West / Wild West** — American desert heritage: terracotta, cacti, denim, western slab serifs. → Ranch/outdoor brands, festivals.

**43. Nautical** — Maritime order: navy/white, stripes, anchors, rope, brass, stencil or classic serifs. → Coastal resorts, seafood, summer campaigns.

## FAMILY 9 — Art-Technique & Conceptual

*Borrowed fine-art techniques; add texture and thought.*

**44. Art Deco** — 1920s geometric glamour: sunbursts, symmetry, jewel tones, gold, flared sans-serifs. → Luxury, cocktail/hospitality, retro-modern UI accents.

**45. Art Nouveau** — Organic flowing ornament: botanical curves, hand-lettering, earthy palette, stained-glass patterning. → Artisan packaging, natural cosmetics, artistic brands.

**46. Surrealism** — Dream logic: impossible juxtaposition, melting forms, unexpected scale. → Concept art, experimental campaigns, literary design.

**47. Pointillism** — Images from dot clusters, optical color mixing, soft grain. → Fine-art branding, editorial texture, education.

**48. Mixed Media** — Photo + illustration + texture collage, analog-digital layering. → Magazines, experimental branding, art education.

**49. Conceptual Sketch** — Raw ideation aesthetic: pencil/ink lines, crosshatch, annotations, paper grain. → Portfolios, process storytelling, brainstorm tools.

**50. Rebus** — Meaning through visual puns and pictogram-word hybrids. → Educational design, clever brand storytelling, puzzles.

## INDUSTRY FIT MAP (project-specific, original)

Use as the FIRST filter when proposing directions:

- **Enterprise / ERP / CRM / Ops tooling** → Base: Utilitarian, Bauhaus. Accent: Bento Box (overview screens), restrained Neo-Brutalism (confident B2B brands). Never: Aurora, Glassmorphism-heavy, any Family 1/7 ornament.
- **POS / Kiosk** → Base: Utilitarian or Bauhaus geometry at large touch scale. Accent: Neo-Brutalism (bold color states = fast recognition). Never: low-contrast Ethereal, decorative anything.
- **Health & Wellness** → Base: Japandi or Wabi Sabi. Accent: restrained Aurora (progress/ambient moments), Light Academia (content-heavy). Never: Cybercore, harsh Brutalism, punitive reds.
- **Fintech / Trading** → Base: Utilitarian precision + strong status hierarchy. Accent: Glassmorphism (card surfaces, sparingly), Neo Frutiger Aero (consumer/playful fintech), Luxury Typography (private banking/wealth). Never: Vaporwave/Synthwave glow on data.
- **Consumer social / Gen Z** → Y2K, Neo Frutiger Aero, Coquette, Kitsch, Memphis — pick one, commit hard.
- **Luxury / Beauty / Fashion** → Luxury Typography base; Neoclassical, Filigree, Art Deco, or Ethereal accents by brand era.
- **Gaming / Entertainment** → Pixel Art, Synthwave, Cybercore, Gothic, Steampunk by genre.
- **Kids / Education** → Kawaii, Anthropomorphic, Scrapbook, Rebus, Pop Art.
- **Food / Hospitality / Local** → Cottagecore, Farmhouse, Nautical, Mid-Century, Bohemian, South West by cuisine and place.
- **Creative agency / Portfolio** → Neo-Brutalism, Brutalism, Mixed Media, Modular Typography, Conceptual Sketch.

## HYBRID RECIPES (dominant + accent, original)

- "Japandi × Bauhaus" — calm enterprise dashboard with rigorous grid.
- "Utilitarian × Neo-Brutalism" — bold internal tooling with personality.
- "Luxury Typography × Art Deco" — premium fintech/wealth landing.
- "Wabi Sabi × Light Academia" — journaling / mental-health product.
- "Bento Box × Neo Frutiger Aero" — friendly consumer SaaS overview.
- "Mid-Century × Pop Art" — warm retail campaign.
- "Gothic × Modular Typography" — music/event identity system.
- "Cottagecore × Scrapbook" — recipe or family-memory app.

## ANTI-SLOP GUARDRAILS FOR STYLE USE (original)

1. **Name it or don't ship it.** Every proposal must cite named styles from this vocabulary. "Modern, clean, minimal" is banned phrasing.
2. **Slop-adjacent styles need justification.** Aurora, Glassmorphism, Synthwave, Cybercore = the raw ingredients of AI-default slop (mesh gradients, neon glow, frosted purple cards). Using them requires a one-line rationale tied to industry and user mental model.
3. **Two-style ceiling.** One dominant + one accent, max. Style soup = slop.
4. **Style ≠ typography excuse.** Naming a style never justifies raw Inter/Roboto. Each style implies a deliberate type pairing — specify it.
5. **Density check.** Ornamental and atmospheric families (1, 2, 7) lose to function in any data-dense screen. They may inform marketing surfaces of the same product while the app itself stays in Family 3.
6. **Trend awareness (2025–26):** Bento Box, Neo-Brutalism, Neo Frutiger Aero, Japandi, and refined Glassmorphism are the current-cycle styles; Families 4–5 are strong for youth/nostalgia campaigns. Classical families are timeless, not trending — use for heritage positioning.
